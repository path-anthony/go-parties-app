## The spine as built

Thirty tables in `go-parties-admin/prisma/schema.prisma`. Every row below Account carries an `account_id` (see the multi-tenancy note). What each one is, in the product's own words:

- **Account**: the company that owns everything below. One row today (GO). Also holds the rush order settings: `min_booking_notice_hours` (default 72) and `rush_contact_phone` (null until set).
- **Item**: anything sellable. The menu. Name, category, nullable price (TBD and custom-quote items exist and are excluded from Ask GO's math), "Billed per" (`price_unit`: a dropdown of Per day, Per hour, Per event, Per weekend, Flat rate, or a short Other of 30 characters at most, enforced by the API; it is customer-facing, so nothing internal goes in it), `source` (Owned or Partner-sourced) and `needs_price_review` (a flag for a supplier price that still needs a look), both admin only and never in a public response, internal notes, photo, and `skills`: the crew skills it needs to run, any number, from the fixed list. Uploaded photos are stored inline as data URLs in `photo_url`. Skills and units are independent: an item can be a physical piece with units, a service with skills (the DJ tiers, no units), or both (a cigar bar that is a real setup and needs waitstaff, a bartender and a photographer).
- **Unit**: one physical instance of an item. The warehouse. A label, a manual status (Available, Booked, Maintenance), and a booking calendar through BookingUnit. Since 2026-09-23 every item that has no skills carries at least one unit (144 got a default "Unit #1" then), so the whole physical catalog is bookable once per date until real counts replace the defaults; the 22 skill-only items were left without units on purpose.
- **Skill**: a crew skill, managed in Settings (add, rename, delete). Twenty-one today: the original eight (DJ/MC, Photographer, Videographer, Photo Booth Attendant, Day-of Coordinator, Waitstaff, Bartender, Live Musician) and thirteen for specialist performers and services. Item.skills, CrewMember.skills and Gig.skill hold the names as text; a rename rewrites them everywhere in one transaction, and a skill can't be deleted while an item or a crew member lists it.
- **CrewMember**: a person who works events. Name, phone, email, a list of skills from that table, an active flag, notes, and whether they agreed to be texted (`sms_consent`, with the date staff recorded it). Admin managed, no login. Deactivating keeps the row and everything it filled.
- **Gig**: one person needed for one skill of one item on one booking. Created automatically, one per skill per unit wanted, the moment a booking takes an item with skills (a three-skill item makes three gigs, each offered and filled on its own); all of them cancelled with the booking; moved with the booking's date. Carries the skill, the event date, the item name (copied, so a renamed or deleted item doesn't rewrite it), a status (Needs Crew, Offered, Filled, Cancelled) and who filled it. Since crew bidding it also holds what a crew member sees on their bid page: a pay range in whole dollars, event type, guest count, start and end time, town and arrival notes, prefilled from the booking where it has them, and a staff switch that pauses its reminders.
- **GigOffer**, **GigQuestion**: a gig put to one crew member, with its own unguessable link to the storefront's `/bid/<token>` page. Status is Sent, Accepted, Declined or Not Selected; the offer also holds the bid (whole dollars, a short note, when it was made), the bidding deadline, and when the crew member confirmed they are set. Inviting crew texts each person their link. Several people can be invited to the same gig; accepting one fills it, marks everyone still in the running Not Selected and tells them, all under a row lock on the gig so an accept and a bid edit can never both win. A second accept on a filled gig is refused by name. A GigQuestion is a question an accepted crew member asked from their page; staff are notified. A link stops working two days after the gig.
- **MessageTemplate**, **MessageLog**, **SmsOptOut**, **AutomationRun**: every automated message is a trigger in `server/triggers.ts` (29 of them, 24 firing today: key, journey lead/client/crew, send class transactional/reminder/nurture, when it sends, when it stops, allowed merge tokens, default SMS and email wording). `message_templates` rows exist only where the admin customized wording or switched a channel off. `message_logs` holds every send attempt with its trigger, journey, a unique idempotency key claimed before sending, how many times it was tried and when it went; statuses are sent, failed, failed_final (gave up after three tries), skipped_disabled, skipped_opted_out, blocked_missing_field, deferred_quiet_hours, deferred_sunday, plus the setup-missing skips. Texts of kind reminder and nurture go 9 AM to 8 PM Eastern only, nurture never on a Sunday. A number that Twilio reports as having replied STOP (or that staff mark) is kept in `sms_opt_outs` and never texted again. Timed messages (lead follow-ups, contract nudge, balance and event reminders, crew reminders) are planned by one pure planner in `server/automation` and sent by an hourly call from n8n; each pass is an `automation_runs` row.
- **Agreement**, **PolicyVersion**, **DesignRequest**, **ContractDocument**: an agreement belongs to exactly one booking or design request and records the policy version, whether the box was ticked and when, and for a storefront customer a keyed hash of their address and their browser's user agent. It also carries the contract: an unguessable signing token, Sent or Signed, when the link first really went out (the 48-hour unsigned reminder counts from there), and the link to the signed PDF. Every new booking must have one, enforced by a deferred database trigger. Policy text is versioned, never edited. A design request is a cart routed to staff because it is over `full_review_threshold`, is for a review occasion, or comes from a package or item category that is; nothing is locked, the customer is texted that a person will be in touch, staff can keep notes on it, and converting it makes a real booking and sends the contract. A ContractDocument is the signed contract, kept for good: the exact text, the PDF bytes, the typed name, time, address and user agent of the signer.
- **Booking**: a confirmed event on a date. Event time and address as free text, customer name, phone and email as separate columns, a stage set by hand (Held by default, Contract Sent, Signed, Completed, Cancelled, plus Released, which only the system sets when an unpaid storefront hold lapses) and a `retainer_paid` flag (hand-ticked; the old `deposit_paid` column is gone). The status people read is computed: Confirmed only when the stage is Signed and the retainer is paid, Retainer Paid when only the retainer is, otherwise the stage. Sending the contract link moves a Held booking to Contract Sent, and a customer signing moves it to Signed. A `rush` flag (event day starts inside the account's minimum notice window when it was made or moved; visibility only), the quoted `total`, and optional links to the Lead it came from, the Customer who made it, and the Package it was booked as. Also: how the balance will be collected and whether it has been, the occasion, and a staff switch that pauses its timed messages.
- **BookingUnit**: which units a booking holds, one row per unit, each carrying the event date. `(unit_id, event_date)` is unique. See the guarantee below.
- **BookingAddon**: what a booking chose for an item. The item name, group name, option name and price change are copied at booking time on purpose: a booking is a record of what was sold, so renaming, repricing or deleting an option later never rewrites it. Quantity is how many units of the item the choice applies to.
- **Customer**: a storefront account. Phone and email (both unique per account), bcrypt password hash, optional name, `phone_verified_at` (column exists, nothing sets it).
- **Package**: a curated bundle of real items with one manual price (not a sum; the gap is Andy's discount). Name, description, price, Draft or Published, one or more occasions (storefront sub-occasion strings; the public feed lists a package under each of them), search keywords (short customer-facing terms, suggested by Claude from the package's name, occasions and items, edited by the admin before saving), a photo. Only Published packages are public, and publishing requires at least one occasion and at least one item. The old single occasion and free-text theme tag became these two lists on 2026-09-24.
- **PackageItem**: the items in a package with a quantity each.
- **AddonGroup**: one question about one item ("Flavor", "Sidewalls"), required or optional, in the order Andy arranged them.
- **Addon**: one answer in a group, with a price change per unit of the item. Zero is a free choice, a negative number is a downgrade.
- **Lead**: the top of the CRM pipeline. Source is one of ask-go (logged when an Ask GO conversation commits to a recommendation), manual (typed in the admin), website (posted by n8n), storefront (created alongside every direct booking), concierge (a "talk to a person" tap on the storefront, on its way to Calendly). Status is the name of a LeadStatus column; tags, notes, occasion, date of interest, theme, and what Ask GO returned. Only manual leads get this app's follow-up messages (day 3 and day 10), and they stop when the lead has a booking or sits in the column named Booked or Lost; a staff switch pauses them. There is still no way to turn a lead into a booking from the lead itself.
- **LeadStatus**: the Leads board's columns, editable in the admin, with a real foreign key so renaming a column cascades to its leads and a column with leads can't be deleted.
- **LeadActivity**: append-only log per lead. Bookings, cancellations, reschedules, item and unit changes and add-on edits write here.
- **Session**, **UsageCounter**, **LoginFailure**: plumbing. Sessions back the admin and customer cookies (logging out deletes the row). Usage counters are per-day counts of things that cost money or can be abused (public Ask GO requests, storefront bookings), kept in the database so a deploy does not reset them. Login failures back the admin login lockout, stored against a keyed hash of the address.

Not in the schema: **Theme** (the package theme tag is gone; search keywords replaced it), **Payment** (nothing is charged by the platform yet; see "Payments and contracts"), and a rate on a crew member (a gig carries a pay range and the accepted bid; payouts stay off-platform). The storefront's original static package flow (`/book/*`, `/held`) was removed on 2026-09-22; every booking a customer can make now goes through the real checkout. The static catalog in `src/data/catalog.ts` survives only as sample data for the `/kit` design page.

## The no-double-booking guarantee

This is load-bearing, not aspirational. Two customers can never hold the same unit on the same date, whichever door the booking came through and whoever edits it afterwards.

- **Database level:** `booking_units (unit_id, event_date)` is unique. Whatever code path tries, Postgres refuses the second row. This is the backstop; everything below exists so customers never hit it.
- **Transaction level:** inside the one transaction that writes the Booking, its BookingUnits, its BookingAddons and the Lead, each unit is selected with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. Two requests racing for the last unit cannot both pick it: the loser sees no row and is told the date is taken. Skipping, not waiting, is deliberate. Under READ COMMITTED a waiter's `NOT EXISTS` check keeps its original snapshot and could still see the unit as free after the winner committed.
- **Several items, and several units of one item:** every unit is locked before anything is written; each lock passes the units the transaction already holds, so a package that wants two of an item gets two different ones. If any item is short by even one unit, the whole transaction rolls back and the 409 names every item that failed and says nothing was booked.
- **Every change reuses the same lock.** A reschedule, from the customer portal or from the admin's booking popup, locks one free unit of each held item on the new date before releasing the old ones, so the booking lands on whichever units are free that day; if any item has none, the booking is left exactly as it was. A change of item locks the new item's unit first. Adding an item to a booking in the admin claims a unit under the same lock. Cancelling deletes the booking's BookingUnit rows, which is what frees the date. The admin's older behaviour (move the same unit ids, refuse if that specific unit was taken) is gone as of 2026-09-21; admin and storefront now have parity.
- **A unit-less physical item can't be promised:** availability reports `directBooking: false` and the booking route refuses it as not-tracked. This is why unit counts from Andy gate real availability for physical inventory.
- **Skills are promised against people.** An item with skills is free on a date only when, for every one of its skills, the active crew members with that skill outnumber the gigs already needing it that day; an item with units and skills also needs a free unit, so its free count is the tighter of the two. Inside the booking transaction, units are locked first (`SKIP LOCKED`, never waiting) and then the crew rows for each skill (`FOR UPDATE`, waiting) in alphabetical skill order across the whole booking, so two transactions that need the same skills always take them in the same order and cannot deadlock, and two bookings racing for the last free person of a skill take turns with exactly one winning. A reschedule re-checks every gig's skill on the new date under the same lock and refuses the whole move if the crew can't cover it; adding an item from the admin's booking popup goes through the same locks. The public catalog and availability report one free count per item whichever bound it.

Verified on the live database, with the race between an admin reschedule and a storefront booking for the last unit of a date: exactly one won, the date ended full, the loser's booking was left whole.

## Pricing as recorded

`Booking.total` is what the customer was quoted, set by the admin, never by the storefront:

- Without a package: each item's price times the units held, plus every add-on choice's price change times the units of that item.
- With a package: the package's bundle price, plus the add-ons. The request must carry `packageId` and exactly the package's items, or it is refused; the package's quantities decide how many units are held.
- Admin edits to a booking (add or remove a unit, change an add-on) move the total by the amount of the edit.
- Null when nothing had a price. Admin-entered bookings carry no total today.

## Public API (no session, rate limited per IP)

- `GET /api/items/public[?q&category&date]`: the catalog as the storefront may show it. With a date, only items with a free unit that day, each with `freeUnits`. Search is on the name only; notes never leave the server.
- `GET /api/items/:id/availability?date=`.
- `GET /api/packages/public?occasion=`: published packages for one sub-occasion, with their items.
- `POST /api/bookings/direct`: `itemId` or `itemIds`, `eventDate`, `customerName`, `phone` and `email` (both required, the account fills them for a signed-in customer), optional `eventTime`, `address`, `packageId`, `addons: { [itemId]: [addonId] }`. Also `occasion`, `agreedToPolicy`, `balancePaymentPreference`, and the bot-check fields `hpField` and `formStartedAt`. A cart that needs review comes back 202 as a design request instead of a held booking.
- `POST /api/recommend`: Ask GO.
- `/api/customer/*`: signup, login, logout, me, bookings, cancel, reschedule, change-item.
- `GET /api/settings/public`: only what the storefront renders: `{ minBookingNoticeHours, rushContactPhone, depositPercentage, cancellationWindowDays, policy }` where `policy` is `{ id, version, text, updatedAt }` or null until text is written. (`depositPercentage` is the retainer share; the key keeps its name.) The review threshold and review occasions are staff rules applied on the server and are not published.
- `GET /api/contracts/:token`, `POST /api/contracts/:token/sign`, `GET /api/contracts/pdf/:downloadToken`: the customer's contract by its unguessable link, signing it, and the signed PDF.
- `GET /api/bids/:token` and its `bid`, `decline`, `confirm` and `question` actions: a crew member's gig page. Built field by field; the address, arrival notes and day-of phone appear only once that person is picked, and the customer's name and contact never do.
- `POST /api/leads/concierge`: logs a "talk to a person" tap before the customer goes to Calendly.
- `POST /api/leads/external`, `POST /api/automations/check-reminders`, `POST /api/webhooks/n8n/send-status`: n8n calling in (website leads, the hourly scheduler pass, delivery reports), shared secret header.

Every public write route has a small body limit, a per-address rate limit and a bot check (a hidden field that must be empty, and a form start time). Ask GO and storefront bookings also have a daily site-wide cap set in Settings. CORS with credentials is given only to these public and customer routes; admin routes refuse requests from any other origin.

Admin only: `POST /api/bookings/staff` (New booking, the same booking code as `POST /api/bookings/direct` plus a quantity per item, an optional existing customer and a confirmed price) and `GET /api/customers?q=`.

Every public item, from the catalog, from Ask GO and from a package, is built by one function (`server/publicItem.ts`, `toPublicItem`) that constructs the object field by field: id, name, category, price, priceUnit, photoUrl, hasUnits, addonGroups. It constructs rather than spreads, so a column added to Item later can't leak by default. This closed a real leak on 2026-09-21: Ask GO had been returning whole item rows, internal notes, account id and timestamps included.

## The three faces

One data model, three doors into it:

1. **Customer storefront** (built, live against the admin). Home is one scrolling screen with no sign-in gate: the photo carousel, then three doors. Ask GO (a conversation that ends in a recommendation with checkboxes), Build your own (Browse: pick a day first, then only what is open that day, a cart, item options answered as each item is added), and the occasion plates (sub-occasion chips, then the published packages for that sub-occasion, each a pre-filled cart). All three land in one checkout: an options step when a batch has anything to configure, date and time with live availability per item, who (name, phone, email, address), an account-or-guest step for guests, held. A customer portal (My party) with sign up, sign in, a real bookings list, cancel, reschedule and change of item. Two pages reached only by a link in a text: the contract (`/sign/<token>`: read, consent, type your name, get the signed PDF) and the crew gig page (`/bid/<token>`, see the third face). A cart that needs a person (over the review amount, or a wedding or corporate occasion) is not held: the customer sees that the team takes it from here and gets a text saying so. Item photos as thumbnails everywhere an item is listed, the photo plate where there is none. Every screen is mobile first at 390px.
2. **Admin command center** (built). Behind one shared password, desktop first, with a real address for every screen and one expandable sidebar built from a single config (`src/nav.tsx`): a section with one screen is a link, a section with several is a group of sub-pages, and there are no in-page tabs. Overview (live KPIs). Leads (a board with drag and drop, configurable columns, tags, an activity log, a detail panel, manual entry, n8n ingestion, and a line on each card saying where it is in the follow-up messages). Scheduling > Bookings and Inventory status. Inventory (compact rows, an item popup with photo drop and the add-on groups editor, quick add, bulk unit creation, search and category filter, CSV import and export). Packages (a modal builder with Draft and Published). Design requests (the review queue, with staff notes and Convert to booking). ## Where AI sits

- **Customer-side (built):** Ask GO is a multi-turn conversation. The storefront sends the whole transcript plus the chosen sub-occasion each turn; the admin calls Claude with the real priced catalog as the only thing it may recommend (public fields only: id, name, category, price and unit; internal notes are never in a prompt), forces a tool call that returns either a clarifying question or a ready recommendation by item id, and logs a Lead only on the ready turn. Ids not in the catalog are dropped server-side. It asks about budget once if it never came up and goes big when the customer declines to give one. Spending is capped: 10 messages per conversation, 500 characters each, 20 requests an hour per visitor, and a daily cap for the public kept in the database (150 by default, set in Settings). Staff use is counted and never limited. The catalog part of the prompt is cached.
- **Admin copilot (partly):** the admin has an Ask GO panel against the same endpoint, and the package builder suggests search keywords (`POST /api/packages/suggest-keywords`); both draw on the same AI budget. The photo-to-listing draft and the "how do I" assistant are not built.
- **Gig matching (no AI):** staff choose who to invite to bid from a checklist of everyone with the skill, and pick from the bids. Automatic matching is not built.

Freedom stays low on pricing and package creation by design. AI drafts, a human approves.

## Sessions and access

- **Admin:** one shared password (`ADMIN_PASSWORD`), a signed `admin_session` cookie backed by a sessions table, every `/api/*` route gated except the public ones listed above, `/api/auth`, `/api/health`, and the n8n routes (shared secret). Five wrong passwords from one address in 15 minutes lock that address out; the count is in the database, so a deploy does not reset it. Admin pages are served with a Content-Security-Policy, HSTS and the usual hardening headers, and admin routes refuse requests from any other origin.
- **Customers:** their own signed `customer_session` cookie, a different name from the admin's, so neither cookie can stand in for the other. Signup and login are rate limited; wrong identifier and wrong password get the same sentence.
- **Storefront calls** to `/api/customer/*` and `/api/bookings/direct` send `credentials: "include"`. Nothing required in a booking request depends on the cookie arriving: name, phone and email always travel in the request, and the cookie only decides whether the booking attaches to the account. In production the customer cookie must be `SameSite=None; Secure` to travel cross-origin, and Safari blocks third-party cookies regardless; so whether signed-in bookings attach to the account in every browser is an open question, while the booking itself succeeds either way. Anything that changes state on these routes must be JSON from an allowed origin.

## Payments and contracts

Contracts are built; payments are not.

- **Retainers and balances:** nothing is charged by the platform. `retainer_paid` and `balance_paid` on Booking are ticked by hand in the booking popup; the retainer share is one percentage in Settings (20 today) and appears on the contract. Balance reminders go out by text and email in the days before the event. A card payment step (Stripe was the direction; SwipeSimple the earlier plan) is the next piece: a link with the contract, a webhook that marks the retainer paid, then balance collection. There is no cancellation fee logic; cancel frees the date and that is all.
- **Contracts:** built in-house, no outside e-sign vendor. The contract is assembled from the booking (parties, event, services, price and payment) and the current policy text, which staff write and version in Settings with merge fields. Staff send the customer a link by text and email (the Send contract link button, or by setting the stage to Contract Sent; converting a design request sends it automatically). Only a real send marks the contract Sent, moves a Held booking to Contract Sent and starts the 48-hour reminder; previewing does none of that. The customer reads it on the storefront (`/sign/<token>`), consents to sign electronically and types their name; the signed PDF is stored in the database with the exact text, time and address, carries GO's own signature block, and is sent to the customer and to staff. Signing is refused once a booking is cancelled, completed or released, and a signing link stops working 30 days after the event. Not built: pasting GO's own contract text word for word as a template.
- **Contractor payouts:** stay off-platform. The platform tracks what's owed and shows an invoice-received view; it does not move money to contractors.

## Marketing and lead tools (adjacent)

n8n stays as it is for the website's lead drip and its own SMS campaigns, and this app never touches those workflows or the Twilio number's inbound webhook. The admin uses n8n for three things: `POST /api/leads/external` (shared secret) takes website leads into the pipeline as source "website"; an hourly call to `POST /api/automations/check-reminders` runs the scheduler; and outgoing email goes through one n8n webhook (`EMAIL_WEBHOOK_URL`), with delivery reported back to `POST /api/webhooks/n8n/send-status`. Texts go straight to Twilio. Only leads typed into the admin get this app's follow-up messages; website leads stay with the n8n campaigns. SMPL CRM is out. Andy approved building the CRM in-house. The exit door is CSV export of every record. Today only Items has it (`GET /api/items/export.csv`, matching the import template). Leads, bookings, units, customers and packages do not yet.

## Multi-tenancy note

Implemented at the schema level: an `Account` table and `account_id` on every other table, one account today, and `getDefaultAccount()` everywhere a route needs one. Whether this becomes a broader Path Intelligence product is still the open product decision it was; the schema no longer stands in the way of either answer.
